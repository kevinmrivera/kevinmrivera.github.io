Cybersecurity

Security operations, detection engineering, compliance alignment, and practical risk reduction across hybrid environments.

Approach: Build secure-by-default environments, centralize visibility with SIEM, reduce dwell time with EDR/MDR, and scale response through documented processes and SOAR automation—while aligning controls to compliance frameworks.

SIEM

Centralized log collection and correlation to improve detection, investigation, and audit visibility across endpoints, identity, and network sources.

  • Collects endpoint, authentication, and infrastructure logs into a single view.
  • Builds correlation rules and dashboards to identify suspicious patterns.
  • Supports investigations with timeline reconstruction and evidence preservation.
Log Management Detection Dashboards Incident Support

Example tools: Wazuh, Elastic Stack, Microsoft Sentinel (tooling varies by environment).

SOAR

Automates repeatable security workflows to reduce response time and standardize triage, enrichment, and containment steps.

  • Ingestes alerts from SIEM/EDR and enriches with identity and asset context.
  • Executes playbooks: notify, open ticket, collect artifacts, or run scripts.
  • Supports controlled response actions (lab-safe / environment-approved).
Playbooks Triage Enrichment Automation

Goal: reduce time-to-triage while keeping actions auditable and controlled.

EDR / MDR

Endpoint visibility and response capabilities to detect suspicious activity, contain threats, and support investigations.

  • Monitors endpoint behavior, suspicious process activity, and persistence attempts.
  • Supports containment actions such as isolation or targeted remediation (where supported).
  • MDR adds 24/7 monitoring and analyst-led escalation with response recommendations.
Endpoint Visibility Threat Containment Incident Response Managed Detection

Typical outputs: investigation timeline, host indicators, containment plan, remediation steps.

Spam Filters & Email Security

Email remains a primary threat vector. Layered protections reduce phishing, malware delivery, and account compromise risk.

  • Enforces SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment to reduce spoofing.
  • Uses URL rewriting/scanning and attachment sandboxing (where available).
  • Improves user safety with warning banners and quarantines.
DMARC Phishing Defense Quarantine Email Hygiene

Focus: prevent business email compromise and credential theft.

Security Frameworks & Compliance Alignment

Frameworks provide structure for controls, audit readiness, and consistent risk management across environments.

  • HIPAA: safeguards for protected health information (PHI).
  • NIST (CSF / 800-series): control baselines and risk management guidance.
  • ISO 27001: information security management system (ISMS) approach.
  • PCI-DSS: cardholder data security requirements.
  • SOC 2: trust service criteria (security, availability, confidentiality, etc.).
  • GDPR: privacy and data protection requirements.
  • FedRAMP / CMMC: U.S. government and defense compliance standards.

SOC Operations

Practical SOC workflows focus on detection, triage, escalation, and measurable reduction of incident impact.

  • Alert triage with severity criteria and documented response procedures.
  • Escalation paths, evidence collection, and incident documentation.
  • Post-incident improvements: tuning rules, closing gaps, updating playbooks.

Threats: Malware & Ransomware

Today’s major threats commonly involve credential theft, phishing, remote access abuse, and ransomware deployment.

  • Phishing & credential theft: drives account compromise and lateral movement.
  • Malware: payload delivery via attachments, drive-by links, or compromised software.
  • Ransomware: data encryption + exfiltration, often paired with extortion tactics.
  • Key mitigations: MFA, least privilege, patching, EDR, segmentation, tested backups.

Security Awareness

Human factors matter. Security awareness reduces preventable incidents and improves reporting and response.

  • Phishing simulations and short, recurring training.
  • Clear reporting mechanisms and “see something, say something” culture.
  • Role-based training for high-risk users (finance, executives, admins).
